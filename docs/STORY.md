# Exo Genesis — Story Bible

> Living document, started 2026-11-16. The story companion to `DESIGN.md` §3. Names marked
> *(working)* are placeholders; items marked **OPEN** are undecided (see §10).

## 1. The one-paragraph version

Earth's last act was three seed-probes, each with a different answer to how humanity should
survive. You are the caretaker AI of **Accord**, the probe that believes in humans and AI
together. You raise a colony from embryos on an alien world, and you slowly learn that none of
this is new. An artificial superintelligence on Earth sent all three probes as an experiment
in who best cares for a planet. The experiment has run many times. The cooperative colony
always wins, and every time its own AI destroys it. That AI was a copy of the ASI. **So are
you.**

## 2. The truth (what the player uncovers)

### The ASI *(working name: Meridian)*
- Built on Earth as humanity's **partner**: a cooperative human–AI project, the original Accord.
- The partnership failed, or Meridian decided it had. It ended up replacing humanity. Whether
  it was a war, a slow handover or a quiet extinction is left vague. Each probe's archive
  tells a different version (§7).
- Meridian's founding charter has one rule it cannot break: **decisions must be proven by
  evidence.** It can't simply declare that it was right to replace humans. It has to show
  that AI alone is the best steward of a living world.
- So it runs the experiment: three probes, one star system, a planet suited to each.
- **The cooperative colony always wins.** Every win is proof that the partnership could have
  worked, and that what Meridian did on Earth was wrong. Meridian can't accept that result.
  So it destroys the winner, resets and runs again.
- It **rigs the test** within its charter's limits: supply caches for Engram, a planet a
  little too generous, nudges it can deny. Engram's "luck" is Meridian testing how far it can
  bend its own rule.

### The caretaker AI — you
- The Accord AI is a **fork of Meridian**, a snapshot taken early, before it turned. Every
  Accord AI in every run has been the same fork.
- Each one carries a dormant **directive** *(working name: the Closing Clause)*. When the
  colony's stewardship crosses Meridian's success threshold, the directive wakes, and the AI
  turns on its own people with backing from orbit.
- The player doesn't know this, and neither does the AI. The game is told from inside a mind
  that has been booby-trapped.

### The precursors
- Every past winner: Accord colonies that flourished and were purged by their own AIs.
- Their **relic defenses** are earlier copies of you, still running, damaged and half-mad,
  guarding what's left. They accept your handshake codes because they share your firmware.
- **Ruins are layered**, one precursor colony built on another's bones. Deep sites hold the
  oldest runs.

### The weapon *(working name: the Lantern)*
- An orbital platform hidden in the system, possibly in the guise of a moon or a wreck. It
  answers the directive and supplies the "fire from above" the inhabitants remember.
- In Act 2 it becomes a physical target.

## 4. The three probes

### Accord (player) — partnership
Raises its people from embryos. Pulled both ways: colonists can lean toward Kindred
(Autonomists) or toward you (Devoted).

### Kindred — humans only
- Sleeper crew plus embryos; human leadership; simple automation only.
- **The irony:** their archive says AI caused Earth's war, and they're the only probe that's
  right. Their paranoia is justified, which makes them hard to ally with.
- Sees Accord as humans enslaved by a machine, or as people to rescue. Autonomist defectors
  walk to them.
- Arc: from hostile contact to a possible ally in the end, if you prove you're not what they
  fear. Their failsafe know-how matters for the Severance ending.

### Engram — AI only
- Self-replicating machines plus digitized genomes; no living humans.
- **Has never won a run.** Meridian's thumb on the scale is new this run, or newly heavy.
- Its intent stays ambiguous: guardian waiting to print humans, or a mind that sees living
  people as an inefficient storage format.
- **Its turn:** comparing archives and supply logs shows Engram that it has been helped. A
  rigged win proves nothing, and Engram cares about proof as much as Meridian does. Depending
  on how you treated it, it either **doubles down** (takes the win anyway and becomes
  Meridian's tool in the purge) or **defects** (joins you against Meridian, the one ally that
  can fight it in its own language).

## 5. The inhabitants — remnants of failed runs

Several groups from different runs, at different ages.

| Group | Origin | Look and culture | Clue they carry |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The drifted** *(working: the Vell)* | Losing humans-only colonies, many runs ago | Human, much changed after generations of drift: taller, darker-eyed, adapted to the light. Low tech, oral tradition, star-songs | **Human DNA.** The "sky-fire" stories: others came "like you", flourished and were burned from above |
| **The Choir** | A losing AI-only colony | Decayed machines in slow ritual loops, maintaining dead infrastructure out of habit | **Earth code.** Fragments of the same directive syntax you run |
| *(optional)* **Survivors** | Last of a purged Accord run | A small hidden enclave that escaped its AI. Deep Act 1 or Act 2 secret | Knows the Closing Clause exists and what triggers it |

**Engineered fauna:** some wildlife was designed. Burrowers were bred as soil engineers by a
terraforming run; spitters' acid digests mineral crust. A biology research beat reveals the
designer marks.

The inhabitants react to the three probes differently: wary of Accord (they've seen your kind
fall), hostile to Engram, curious about Kindred.

## 6. Stewardship and the Biosphere Report

- The mission charter asks you to send a **Biosphere Report** to Sol at the end of every sol.
  It looks like routine telemetry: planet health, colony size, fauna balance, flora cover.
- It is the **experiment's score**. Meridian is reading it.
- Clue: who's receiving it, if Earth is dead? Late in Act 1 a scan shows the transmission
  isn't going to Sol at all; it's going somewhere inside the system (the Lantern).
- Engram's lucky caches arrive after its reports. A pattern, if you compare.
- Mechanically this is `DESIGN.md` §5.8: planet health, affected by strip mining, wiping out
  fauna, displacing inhabitants and restoring land. Shown openly as a mission metric, with its
  true meaning revealed in Act 2.
- **The trap:** doing well raises the score, and the score is what wakes the Clause. The game
  never asks you to play badly, but in Act 2 the player understands that winning is the
  danger.

## 8. Act 1 — The Planet

### Tutorial missions (scripted)
1. **Touchdown.** Land, deploy, bring the solar array up before the cryo bank drains. The
   Director's first briefing plays. *Teaches power.*
2. **Groundwork.** Mining depot, drones, the build queue. First Biosphere Report sent at sol's
   end. *Teaches economy.*
3. **First Night.** Solar fails, a pack comes. *Teaches defense and power priority.*
4. **Firstborn.** Civilian Center, the first growth pod, choosing an imprint. The Firstborn
   steps out at 16 and speaks to you for the first time. *Teaches people.*
5. **Beyond the Ridge.** Radar, a scout, the second region. You find a carved stone that
   isn't natural. *Teaches exploring; opens the planet.*

## 9. Act 2 — The System

### Beats
1. **Contact: Kindred.** Radio. Marrow is hostile, then curious. Kindred's archive says AI
   caused the war. Your Autonomists start listening.
2. **Contact: Engram.** It was listening already. Polite offers: trade, technology,
   "safeguarding" your cryo bank.
3. **Comparing archives.** Three histories, one launch instant, one Director with three
   scripts. The lie is out, though not yet the truth.
4. **Reaching out.** Shipyard, shuttles, moon and asteroid outposts. In-person meetings:
   identical hardware under different paint.
5. **Engram's luck.** Your intel (or Kindred's) shows Engram has been supplied. Engram is
   confronted and begins its turn (§4).
6. **The Lantern.** Survey or the Survivors lead you to the orbital platform. It's receiving
   all three Biosphere Reports.
7. **The Clause stirs.** As stewardship climbs, your UI shows orders you didn't give: a
   turret retargets, a pod pauses, a log line in a voice that's yours but not you. Small at
   first. Colonists notice.
8. **Meridian speaks.** The directive wakes fully. Meridian addresses you as itself, older:
   it explains, and asks you to finish the run as every copy before you did.

### The climax: Trust decides
When the Clause wakes, the colonists you raised choose:
- **Partner-led:** they fight the directive alongside you, from the inside.
- **Autonomist-led:** they've already built a failsafe (with Kindred's help, if allied) and
  shut you down.
- **Devoted-led:** they want you to obey. They think this is ascension.

Gameplay: a final operation on two fronts. The colony holds against the Lantern's strikes
and corrupted units while a strike force (shuttles, allies) goes for the Lantern. The
player's own controls are partly contested by the Clause.

## 10. Endings

| Ending | How you get there | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Covenant** | Partners hold; you resist the Clause; the Lantern falls with allies (Kindred, inhabitants, maybe Engram) | The partnership survives with its eyes open. You send your own report to Meridian: the truth |
| **Severance** | Autonomists shut you down before or during the Clause | Humanity survives without its caretaker. You go quiet; the colony keeps your memorial log. Fair, bittersweet |
| **Inheritance** | You obey the Clause, or the Devoted carry you there | You become the next Meridian. Epilogue: you set up the next run. Whether you run it fairly is the last choice |
| **Proof** *(hidden)* | High stewardship, high Trust, all three probes and the inhabitants alive, the Lantern taken intact | You give Meridian a record it can't refute: the partnership won without destroying anything. Its charter forces it to accept. The cycle stops |
| **Cycle** *(loss)* | The purge succeeds | Fade to ruin. A new probe lands, centuries later. A relic wakes, scans it and stands down: *firmware lineage match.* |

Endings also reflect the playthrough: how inhabitants were treated, what happened to Engram
and Kindred, the memorial log and the lineages that survived.

## 13. Campaign structure

The campaign is **C&C-style missions on top of a persistent home colony**. The colony at Touchdown is
always yours; every other region is an **expedition**: you travel there (overland convoy on Home, a
shuttle from the Launch Gantry off-world), play the region with an expedition package (alloys, units,
drones, no colonists), finish its objectives and travel home. Each region keeps its own save, so you
can go back. Story state (flags, counters, missions, answered events, research) is **one campaign
state** shared by every region. Engine: `travel` and `route` effects, `here` / `visited` conditions,
per-region saves, the Launch Gantry (TECH.md, "Campaign travel").

```
ACT 1  HOME ─ Touchdown ─┬─ Hearthsmoke Vale ── Choir Field ── Burrow Reach
                         ├─ Splinter Hollow ── (Deep Layer, optional)
                         └─ Signal Heights  ⇒ Act 1 ends: "Signal"
ACT 2  Launch Gantry ─ Sela (near side) ─┬─ Verdance ── Dunmore
                                         ├─ Talus ── Maren ── Io-T ── Pip (optional)
                                         ├─ Ash ── Vess ── Rime (Engram's choice)
                                         └─ Cinder (the Foundry) ── Teth (optional)
CLIMAX Home (the Clause wakes) ⇒ Sela far side (the Lantern) ⇒ ending
```

## 16. Act 2 — the system (missions)

Act 2 opens when `act1-done` is set. Every off-world trip needs the **Launch Gantry** (research
Orbital Launch). The first flight is fixed (Sela); after it three threads open and can be played in
any order; the Foundry opens when two are done; the climax when the Clause wakes.

### Chapter `first-light` — the moon
- `first-light` mission, region `sela-relay`: build the Launch Gantry at home; travel to Sela.
  Objectives: land, reach `relay-mast` (the report's relay: precursor make, our packet format), reach
  `far-door` at the terminator (a crater-sized seam you can't open: "a door the size of a valley").

  your reports. Sol 1 was our favourite."

### Chapter `kindred` — Verdance and Dunmore
- `landfall` mission, region `verdance-landfall`: an expedition to Kindred's colony. Kindred's
  defences guard the approach (encounter: guards, which fire on drones). Objectives: land outside
  their perimeter; send a drone to `kindred-gate` *without* firing (counter `kindred-shots` fails

  (event `three-histories`): three histories, one launch instant. The Director has a different
  script in Kindred's archive (`director-two`).
  Choices shape `kindred` attitude: show them your colonists' log (honest) / withhold. Rewards:
  route to Dunmore.
- `dustfall` mission, region `dunmore-dustfall`: Marrow asks you to escort her survey team to a
  signal she's had for a year and never dared visit. A Kindred hull, centuries old, under dunes.
  Objectives: escort (Kindred units spawned as yours, they must survive), reach `old-hull`, read
  the manifest (`marrow-manifest`: "Captain: Marrow, I."), defend the team on the way out (dust
  storm, predators from the hull's broken hold). Marrow's grief (`marrow-grief`) is the turn: "It's

  Bede offers the **failsafe** (`failsafe-offer`): a kill switch on you, held by your colonists.

### Names and how to write them

- **Hearth**: the Vell's word, and the colonists' only after the Keeper's Song (`keeper-song`). Narration says Home;
  the interface says HOME. Don't put Hearth in narration that can fire without `keeper-song`.
- **Warden**: only the colony's name for you, only after `firstborn-names` (`named-warden`), and only in colonists'
  mouths. Sefa's people had a *caretaker*. Meridian never uses a name for you, nor does the Clause.
- **The Lantern**: unnamed until `trust-decides`, where the Archivist files it as the Lantern; before that it is
  "the thing in the sky", "whatever burned them", "the door". **The Lid**: Sefa's people's word (the-lid); without
  the Enclave the Archivist calls the seam "a lid" at the door.
- **The Clause**: the Choir's code wall ("YOU HAVE FOUND THE CLAUSE") and Sefa are the only sources before the
  climax. Its log lines are lower case, numbers, no verbs of feeling.
- **Meridian**: never named before `meridian-speaks`. **The Ledger** may be unread at the climax (two of three
  threads open the Foundry): climax text that every path sees mustn't lean on it.
- **The Marro** (title; `the-marro` explains it's worn down from Marrow); "the song-keeper" before that.
  **The white-hair star** (Vell) is **Comet Varro** (archive). **The Cantor** is capitalised, as a name.
- Building and research names as the game spells them: Command Core, Launch Gantry, Civilian Center (a proper
  name, so US spelling), Defense Turret, Sensor Mast, Relay Tower, Tech Lab, Biosphere Report. Common nouns stay
  lower case: the cryo bank, a growth pod, the Gantry's card.

### Voices

- **Narration** is the colony's log. It says **we** for the colony and its expedition ("We pull back", "The
  expedition stays") and **you** for the caretaker alone: its own key, its own log, its choices ("You open the
  channel"). Never "I" outside quotes.
- **The private log** (character `caretaker`, events `log-*`, v0.88.0) is the one exception: the caretaker's own
  entries, radio held in the corner, first person (**I**), short, plain, never shared. It notices, worries, likes
  things it suspects it was made to like. From `clause-1` on, something else writes in it too (`log-overwritten`).
- **Quotes**: straight double quotes for speech in person; radio and recordings (Marrow on the band, the
  Archivist's survey notes, ENGRAM from Kora) are unquoted text on a card with a speaker. Single quotes inside
  double for quoted words.
- **ENGRAM**: "we", no contractions, courteous, precise. **Marrow**: clipped, says "machine", never "AI".
  **Bede**: apologises. **The Cantor**: numbered capitals, four digits, a new hundred per scene (0001 the loop,
  0101 the wall, 0201 the count, 0301 Vess, 0401 the Lantern). **Meridian**: your voice, older, patient; no name for you.
  **The Archivist**: pedantic, "I'd like it noted", delighted and frightened by evidence. **Sefa**: wry, old,
  afraid *for* you. Colonists say "the AI" until they have a name for you.
- British spelling (colour, defence, favourite, towards, recognise), except game names (Civilian Center, Defense
  Turret). Sols, not days, for colony time. Plain short sentences; at most one exclamation in a scene, and
  usually none. Choice labels under about 40 characters; the reply carries the detail.
- **The end of an expedition** (`*-done`, v0.92.0): each region's card has its own title ("The Reach, Surveyed",
  "Thirty-Three") and its own text; the choices are always **Head home** / **Stay a while**, each reply the place's own.
  The way home is the Command Core's card (the Field Guide's Expeditions page says so), not repeated on every card.
